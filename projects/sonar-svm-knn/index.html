Sonar mines vs rocks: SVM and KNN, tuned two ways

Two classifiers, two hyperparameter optimizers and two train-test splits, compared on 208 sonar signals.

Type: Course project, Machine LearningTeam: With M. Daffa Naufal M.Data: Connectionist Bench (Sonar, Mines vs Rocks), UCI
208observations, 60 features each
92.86%best test accuracy (KNN, 80:20)
92.06%best at 70:30 (SVM, Bayesian)
84.13%lowest (KNN, genetic, 70:30)

The question

Can sonar echoes tell a mine from a rock, and does the answer depend on the classifier, on how the hyperparameters are tuned, or on how the data is split? The dataset has 97 rock and 111 mine signals.

Method

  • Two classifiers: Support Vector Machine (SVM) and K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN).
  • Two optimizers for their hyperparameters: Bayesian Optimization and a Genetic Algorithm.
  • Two splits: 80:20 (166 training and 42 test cases) and 70:30 (145 training and 63 test cases).
  • Compared test accuracy across all combinations.

Results

  • 80:20 split. KNN was best at 92.86% (K = 1, p = 2), ahead of SVM at 90.48%.
  • 70:30 split. SVM with Bayesian Optimization was best at 92.06%. KNN with the Genetic Algorithm was lowest at 84.13%.
  • Overall. KNN reached the highest single score, while SVM was more consistent across optimizers and splits. Both optimizers found good parameters, and Bayesian Optimization was ahead in several scenarios.

Read with care

Limitations

  • With only 42 or 63 test cases, one extra mistake moves accuracy by roughly 2 percentage points, so small gaps between methods are not conclusive.
  • The comparison uses two splits only. Repeated or cross-validated runs would give a steadier comparison.
